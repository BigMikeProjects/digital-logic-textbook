Definitions
D or Esc closes

            

                
m
A one-clock pulse: the button M after the synchronizer and debouncer (see Switch Debouncer). On m the laser fires and our unit starts counting.

                
r
A one-clock pulse from the receiver subsystem when the reflected light comes back.

                
Coarse count
The number of whole clock periods between firing and the echo: what register C counts.

                
Fine count
Where inside the last, unfinished period the echo arrived, in small equal steps (here 32 steps of 1.95 ns in a 62.5 ns period).

                
Time-to-digital converter (TDC)
The extra timing circuitry that measures that fine part. Time = coarse × T + fine × T / 32.

                
1 m per clock
At 300 MHz one clock lasts T = 3.33 ns. Light covers 3 × 108 m/s × 3.33 ns = 1 m in that time. Out and back, a flag d meters away takes 2d clocks.

                
C, Dist
Register C counts clocks in COUNT (10 bits, 0 to 1,023). Register Dist holds the last result, Dist ← C >> 1: half the count, made by wiring C’s upper nine bits to Dist, no adder. The same register, adder, mux and comparator pattern as Timer Circuit.

                
tc
Status: 1 when C = 1,023, all ones. No echo came in time; the timeout wins if r arrives in the same clock.

                
clr, ldDist, disp
Moore control outputs. clr = 1 sets C to 0 (IDLE, LOAD, SHOW); ldDist = 1 loads Dist (LOAD); disp = 1 means there is a result to show (SHOW); the display unit blanks itself after 5 s on its own. Built like the controller in Traffic-Light-Controller.
