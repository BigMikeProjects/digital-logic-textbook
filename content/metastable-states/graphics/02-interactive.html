A metastable state cannot be designed away. Anything that stores a bit has two stable states, and there is always a balance point between them. What a designer can do is give it time: each extra bit of waiting makes an unsettled output much less likely. That is what Synchronizers does.

decision windowfrom tsu before the clock edge to th after it; D must not change inside it. Here it is 1.5 ns wide. tsusetup time — how long D must be steady before the clock edge. 1 ns here. thhold time — how long D must stay steady after the clock edge. 0.5 ns here. tcoclock-to-output delay — from the clock edge until Q shows the new value. 1 ns here. metastable stateQ balanced between 0 and 1, neither a valid 0 nor a valid 1. It does not last: Q hovers near halfway, wobbling, for a while, then settles to one of them. settling timealso called resolution time — how long a metastable Q takes to become a clean 0 or 1, measured here from the clock edge. It cannot be predicted, and it is usually short.
