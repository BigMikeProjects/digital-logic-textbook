Bits Come in Bunches
click the switch · that is the entire vocabulary of digital electronics
A bit is a single binary digit — a 0 or a 1. Nothing else exists.
The whole alphabet:
0
1
— that’s it.
Why two? Hardware is built from switches. A switch is OFF (0) or ON (1) — two states that are cheap to build and easy to tell apart, even in a noisy circuit.
Everything digital — numbers, text, pictures, this page — is bits in patterns. The rest of this course is about giving those patterns meaning.

Counting is the odometer idea: when a column runs out of symbols it rolls back to 0 and carries. Decimal runs out at 9 — binary runs out at 1, so it carries constantly. Watch 0111 → 1000 ripple.
To read a binary number, add the place values under the 1s and skip the 0s — that’s all the sum line is doing. You’ll get fluent at converting (both directions, two methods) in Binary To/From Decimal.
