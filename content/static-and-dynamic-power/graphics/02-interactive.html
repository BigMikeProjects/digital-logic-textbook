A footnote: the short-circuit current
While the input is passing between 0 and 1, both transistors conduct for a moment and a little current flows straight from VDD to ground. At the logic levels only one is on; during the edge, briefly, both are. With fast edges it is small, so this graphic leaves it out.

From energy per edge to power
Pdynamic = α · C · VDD2 · f
= (C·VDD2) × (α·f): the energy of one spike × spikes per second.
Where 5 nA comes from
Non-ideal Switching modeled an off transistor as about 1 GΩ: at 5 V that leaks 5 V / 1 GΩ = 5 nA, or 25 nW per gate.
Real leakage is not a plain resistor: it depends mostly on the transistor’s threshold voltage and rises quickly with temperature. So designers quote a leakage current per gate, and this graphic holds it at 5 nA.

The static term
Pstatic = N · Ileak · VDD
N = the number of gates. It does not depend on f or α: the chip pays it doing nothing.
This is the term that sets a phone’s standby time. The fix is power gating: cut the supply to a block that is idle.

The meter — the total split into its two terms
To cut dynamic power α C VDD2 f
  • Lower VDD: the best lever, because the supply is squared. 1.0 → 0.8 V cuts dynamic power to .
  • Lower f: power falls in step with the clock, but so does the work done per second.
  • Gate the clock: stop the clock to a block that is idle, so its α drops to 0.
  • Shrink C: shorter wires and smaller loads.
To cut static power N Ileak VDD
  • Power-gate idle blocks: cut their supply, so they leak nothing at all.
  • Lower VDD: helps here too, but only in proportion. 1.0 → 0.8 V cuts static power to .
  • Choose a low-leakage process: transistors that leak less, usually at some cost in speed.
  • Slowing the clock or gating it does nothing for this term.
Halve one quantity, starting from the defaults — new power ÷ old
Halving VDD quarters the dynamic power. No other single quantity does as much, and dynamic is nearly all of the total at these settings.
The biggest lever is not free
Lowering VDD shrinks the gap between a 0 and a 1, so there is less noise margin, and every gate switches more slowly.

Definitions
D or Esc closes
Dynamic power
The cost of changing outputs: Pdynamic = α C VDD2 f. Each 0 → 1 takes C VDD2 from the supply; half is stored on C, half is heat in the PMOS. The 1 → 0 turns the stored half into heat in the NMOS.
Static power
The cost of holding outputs: Pstatic = N Ileak VDD, from the small current that leaks through every off transistor. Paid whether or not anything switches.
C, load capacitance
Everything the output has to charge: the inputs of the gates it drives, plus the wire. A few femtofarads (fF, 10−15 F) per gate.
α, activity factor
The fraction of clock cycles in which this output goes 0 → 1. A clock net has α = 1; a typical logic node is near 0.1.
fJ, fF
Femtojoule and femtofarad: 1 fJ = 10−15 J, 1 fF = 10−15 F. The energy and the capacitance of one gate are this small.
f, VDD, N
The clock frequency, the supply voltage, and the number of gates in the block.
Ileak
The leakage current of one idle gate, in nanoamps. In Non-ideal Switching it came from a 1 GΩ off resistance.
Crossover frequency
The clock frequency at which the two terms are equal: f = Ileak / (α C VDD). Below it, static power is the larger one.
Clock gating
Stopping the clock to an idle block so nothing in it switches. It cuts dynamic power only.
Power gating
Disconnecting an idle block from the supply so it cannot leak. It cuts static power; the block forgets its state unless it is saved first.
Header (sleep switch)
One PMOS between VDD and a block, driven by a control signal sleep. sleep = 0: on, the block runs. sleep = 1: off, the block is cut from the supply.
Short-circuit current
The brief current straight from VDD to ground while an input is between 0 and 1 and both transistors conduct. Small with fast edges; left out here.
