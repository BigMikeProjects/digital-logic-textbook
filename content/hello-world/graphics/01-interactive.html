▶ STEPS
design.sv describes
A module is hardware: a named block with a boundary of ports and logic inside. It doesn’t run, print, or explain itself — it just is the circuit.
testbench.sv exercises
The bench is your lab station: it drives the inputs, watches the outputs, and prints what it sees. Every $display line in the console came from the bench — the design printed nothing.
The truth table judges
You knew what XOR should do before running anything. The bench’s output is compared against that expectation — the truth table is the answer key.
Hardware has no print statement, and you can’t ask a circuit “are you correct?” — the testbench is how a design gets interrogated. No bench, no evidence.
In the labs: every design you build ships as this pair — write the module, write the bench, run, and compare the output against what the truth table says it must be. This two-file skeleton carries through every design in the course.
