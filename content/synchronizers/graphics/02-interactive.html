Synchronizers — two flip-flops and a clock period of waiting

An outside input can land in the decision window. A synchronizer gives it a clock period to settle before anything else looks.

Both are counted from the clock edge. 9 ns = T − tsu. No tco comes off it, because no logic follows FF1.
The honest limit: a hover that lasted past 9 ns would still reach FF2 unsettled. That is extremely rare. A synchronizer makes the risk negligible, not zero.
The cost: latency — Q2 follows A one to two clocks late
Move the change by 2.5 ns and the answer arrives a whole clock later: 12.5 ns against 20 ns. You cannot choose which. For buttons and sensors the wait does not matter: a press lasts millions of clock periods.

The coin sensor L in Level-to-Pulse FSM is exactly this kind of input. The rule in Mealy versus Moore, Moore unless the inputs are known to be synchronized, is this topic applied.

When the clock is very fast, a third flip-flop adds one more full period of settling time.

1Synchronize every asynchronous input
Buttons, switches, sensors, signals from another clock: none of them goes straight into a state machine.
