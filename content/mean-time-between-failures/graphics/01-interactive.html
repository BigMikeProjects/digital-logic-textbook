The bottom stays fixed: 10,000 catches a second (0.1 ns × 100 MHz × 1 MHz). Only the top moves. One more nanosecond is five more τ, so the top grows by e5 ≈ 148. On a scale of tens, ×148 is always the same distance: the needle slides in a straight line.

A synchronizer does not stop the first flip-flop from being caught. It gives it a whole clock period to settle.
Faster clock
The period halves to 5 ns, so the synchronizer’s first flip-flop gets only 3 ns to settle, and the clock samples twice as often.
Many units
A product shipped in a million copies fails somewhere a million times as often.
The remedy is the same
More settling time. A third flip-flop at 200 MHz gives 2 × (5 − 2) = 6 ns: about . Far better than 2.72 minutes, though not the 746,000 years of the slower clock.

Definitions
D or Esc closes
MTBF
Mean time between failures: the average time from one synchronizer failure to the next. Failures are random, so this is a rate, not a date: a 746,000-year MTBF can still fail tomorrow, though that is very unlikely.
metastable state
The flip-flop’s output hovers near halfway between 0 and 1, wobbling, for a while, then settles to one of them. See Metastable States.
caught
The input changed so close to a clock edge that the flip-flop could not cleanly decide, so it may hover. A catch is not yet a failure: it fails only if it is still hovering when the next flip-flop reads it.
window T0
How close to the edge a change must land to catch the flip-flop. Here 0.1 ns, far narrower than the setup-to-hold decision window: most changes inside that window still resolve at once.
settling time tr
How long the design lets a caught flip-flop settle before anything reads it: the clock period(s) minus tco + tsu (2 ns here) minus any logic in the path. A synchronizer’s first flip-flop gets 10 − 2 = 8 ns. See Synchronizers.
τ (tau)
The settling time constant of the flip-flop. Every τ of waiting cuts the chance of still hovering by a factor of e ≈ 2.7. Here 0.2 ns, a round, older-technology value; a modern part’s τ is tens of picoseconds.
fclk, fdata
How often the clock samples (100 MHz) and how often the input changes (1 MHz). All constants here are illustrative round values, not a data sheet. The full timing picture is in Practical Considerations Overview.
