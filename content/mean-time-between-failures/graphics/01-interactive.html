What to notice
About 15 changes in 100 land in the decision window, and almost all of those are decided at once. Only those in the thin slice right at the edge, about 1 in 100, catch the flip-flop and make it hover. Why it hovers is in Metastable States.

Two names
τ (tau) belongs to the flip-flop: how fast a hover dies out. A faster flip-flop has a smaller τ.
tr belongs to the design: how long it waits before anything reads the flip-flop. tr is the slack: what is left of the clock period after tco, the logic and tsu.
Mean time between failures — where the number comes from, and where it is used today
How it fits here
A synchronizer makes the risk negligible, not zero (see Synchronizers). The mean time between failures says how negligible, in years, so a designer can show a system is reliable enough.

Definitions
D or Esc closes
external input
A signal from outside the circuit, such as a button or a sensor. It changes whenever it likes, with no regard for the clock.
decision window
From the setup time before a clock edge to the hold time after it (here −1 to +0.5 ns). A change inside it is not guaranteed to be read cleanly.
caught, T0
A change so close to the edge that the flip-flop cannot decide at once. T0 is how wide that slice is: here 0.1 ns, a small part of the decision window.
hover
A caught flip-flop’s output sits near halfway, wobbling, then settles to 0 or 1: a metastable state. See Metastable States.
resolution time tr
How long the design lets a caught flip-flop settle before anything reads it. tr is the slack: what is left of the clock period after tco, the logic and tsu, so a synchronizer’s first flip-flop gets 10 − 2 = 8 ns. Each added flip-flop stacks one more period of slack (a simplification: each stage’s slack adds in the exponent, which real chains follow only approximately).
τ (tau)
The flip-flop’s settling time constant: every τ, the chance of still hovering drops by e ≈ 2.7. Here 0.2 ns; a faster flip-flop has a smaller τ.
MTBF
Mean time between failures: the average time from one failure to the next. A rate, not a date. See Synchronizers.
fclk, fdata
How often the clock samples (100 MHz) and how often the input changes (1 MHz). All constants here are illustrative round values, not a data sheet.
